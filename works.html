I've worked on three projects that reflect my journey into design.

Project 1 is a Refugee Support App that helps newcomers access local services.

Project 2 is a Portfolio Website Redesign, where I applied core UX principles to improve structure and accessibility.

Project 3 is a passion project — a fictional animal shelter site called “Rescue Me”, designed to make pet adoption easier and more engaging.

Design, to me, is more than just making things look good — it's about creating experiences that are intuitive, meaningful, and accessible. In all of my projects, I focus on the human side of design. I aim to understand the users' needs, reduce friction, and bring clarity through structure, flow, and visual hierarchy.

Whether I'm building tools for vulnerable communities or crafting personal projects, I always try to approach my work with empathy, creativity, and purpose. My background in language and communication taught me the importance of how people receive and process information. I bring this sensitivity into my design process — I try to anticipate what users might think, feel, or struggle with at each step. I ask myself: What do they need right now? What could confuse or slow them down? And how can I make this experience more comfortable, more welcoming, and more empowering?

Whether I'm building a tool for vulnerable communities, redesigning my own site, or working on a creative concept just for fun, I always start from the user's perspective. I value honest feedback, thoughtful iteration, and purposeful design choices. Each of my projects has taught me something new — about design, about people, and about myself as a designer.

Project 1: Refugee Support App

This app was inspired by my time working with refugees in Canada. I designed a simple, multilingual interface that helps users find essential services like housing, legal aid, and healthcare. The focus was on clarity, trust, and accessibility — especially for users with limited tech experience or language skills. I used user feedback to shape the structure and ensured that information was easy to access in just a few taps. This project emerged from my experience working closely with refugee families in Canada. I noticed a common challenge: people arriving in a new country often feel overwhelmed trying to access services. So I designed a mobile-first platform that guides users step-by-step through options for healthcare, housing, legal aid, and language classes. I prioritized simplicity, used icons to support limited literacy, and made sure everything worked well in multiple languages. The final prototype was tested with real users and iterated based on their feedback.

Project 2: Portfolio Website Redesign

For this project, I redesigned my personal portfolio site — the one you're currently viewing. My goal was to create a user-friendly experience that showcases my skills and projects. I applied key UX principles such as hierarchy, contrast, and responsive design. I tested several layouts in Figma and adjusted based on feedback from peers. The result is a clean, clear, and professional site that reflects who I am as a designer. As someone entering the field of design, I wanted my website to reflect both my personality and my process. I started by sketching ideas on paper, then translated them into digital wireframes using Figma. I chose a calm, clean visual language with gentle colors and clear spacing to make the content feel approachable. I structured the site around storytelling — showing not just what I built, but why I built it. This project helped me understand responsive design, content prioritization, and how to create a visual voice that feels authentic.

Project 3: “Rescue Me” - Animal Shelter Website

“Rescue Me” is a fictional project close to my heart. I created a joyful, inviting website for a dog adoption center. The site includes a searchable list of adoptable dogs, filters for age/size, and helpful resources for new pet owners. I focused on emotional design, using warm visuals and friendly language to connect users with animals in need. This project allowed me to explore storytelling and usability in a creative, mission-driven way. “Rescue Me” is a fictional dog shelter project I created to explore emotional design. The challenge was to make adoption easier, but also more personal. I focused on the emotions behind pet adoption — connection, hope, joy — and used warm tones, soft typography, and friendly copywriting. Visitors can browse available dogs using filters for age, breed, and temperament. Each dog has its own profile page with photos, stories, and adoption steps. This project was a great exercise in designing for trust and action.
